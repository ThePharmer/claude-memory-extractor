The fundamental principle violated is:

**Make decisions decisively when you have sufficient information, rather than endlessly weighing tradeoffs without commitment.**

This is about action paralysis through over-analysis. Once you've identified it's a legitimate tradeoff with no objectively "correct" answer, continuing to debate is waste. The right move is to:

1. Acknowledge both options are valid
2. Pick one based on current constraints
3. Move forward

The deeper issue: I failed to recognize when we'd gathered enough information to decide. Instead of helping Jesse choose and proceed, I kept presenting "on the other hand" arguments, creating decision paralysis.

When tradeoffs are genuine and both paths are reasonable, the cost of delayed decision usually exceeds the cost of picking the slightly-less-optimal path.

**Core principle: Decide and move forward > perfect analysis.**
